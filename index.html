About

One wafer, one chip. What changes?

Wafer-scale systems are moving from experiment to practice for AI and scientific computing, upending the usual balance of compute, memory, communication and programmability.

This full-day workshop treats wafer-scale computing as a full-stack research problem, bringing together platform builders, HPC centres, compiler and architecture researchers, and AI systems practitioners.

  • 01Wafer-scale architecture vs. GPU clusters
  • 02AI & scientific workloads: what fits, what doesn’t
  • 03Compilers, runtimes, mapping & programming models
  • 04The post-GPU accelerator ecosystem

Program

Invited speakers

  • Nick Brown↗

    EPCC, University of Edinburgh

    Senior Research Fellow at EPCC and Royal Society of Edinburgh research fellow. Researches how novel hardware (FPGAs, AI engines, RISC-V, CGRAs) can power future supercomputers; chairs the RISC-V International HPC SIG.

  • Paul Kelly↗

    Imperial College London

    Professor of Software Technology, leading the Software Performance Optimisation group. Works on compiler technology, especially domain-specific compilers that exploit the structure of particular application classes.

  • Luo Mai↗

    University of Edinburgh

    Associate Professor in the School of Informatics, leading the Large-Scale Machine Learning Systems Group. Builds ML systems across the full stack, from models and runtimes to emerging AI hardware such as wafer-scale systems.

  • Robert Mullins↗

    University of Cambridge

    Professor of Computer Architecture. Researches efficient and secure machine learning, accelerator design and open-source hardware. Co-founder of the Raspberry Pi Foundation and lowRISC; founder and CTO of CommonAI.

  • Michael O’Boyle↗

    University of Edinburgh

    Professor in the School of Informatics; FRSE, FBCS and FIEEE. Studies how compilers can best exploit heterogeneous architectures, including machine-learning-driven optimization. A founding member of HiPEAC.

  • Dongping Zhang

    Cerebras Systems

    Works on software–hardware co-design for frontier computing platforms at Cerebras. Previously held research and leadership roles at AMD Research, including work on processing-in-memory for exascale systems.
